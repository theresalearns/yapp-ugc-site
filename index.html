FAQ

Fair questions

Who owns the creator's likeness?

The creator, always. Every avatar is built with recorded consent and a licensing agreement. Brands license usage per campaign, per platform, per period — and creators can see and revoke where their face appears. See the Likeness Ledger.

How is this different from HeyGen or Argil?

Those are tools — you still write scripts, cast avatars, and produce everything yourself. Yapp UGC is a production layer: you send a brief, our AI producer and licensed creator network return finished, rights-cleared content.

What if the content misses our brand voice?

Every brief gets a one-click brand profile the AI producer learns from — banned words, tone, and compliance rules for regulated categories like finance and health. You approve drafts before anything renders at volume.

How do creators get paid?

Per video used, weekly, in their local currency, straight to their account — no invoices, no 60-day terms. Rates are set by the creator and shown upfront to brands.

Every render logged.

The Likeness Ledger is the public record behind every video we produce. No other UGC or AI-avatar platform has one. This is why brands can run our content safely — and why creators trust us with their face.

1

Consent on record

Every avatar starts with a recorded consent session — that recording is the legal likeness license. Stored, auditable, and tied to every render.

2

Per-use logging

Every video gets a ledger entry: whose face, which brand, which platform, what period. Creators are paid from the ledger — per use, weekly.

3

Revocable, always

Creators can pause or revoke their license anytime. Revoked faces stop being licensed for new renders — immediately, platform-wide.
